Dr. Neil Shneider, MD, PhD, is the Claire Tow Associate Professor of Motor Neuron Disorders in Neurology and the director of the Eleanor and Lou Gehrig ALS Center at Columbia University Medical Center. As a physician-scientist with expertise in neuromuscular development and disease, Dr. Shneider is a leader in the research and treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), also known as Lou Gehrig’s disease. Dr. Shneider has developed basic, translational and clinical research programs to characterize the mechanisms of ALS-causing mutations and, in turn, inform the development and use of investigational ALS therapies in the clinic.

Research from the Shneider lab has demonstrated that mutations in the FUS gene, which cause FUS-ALS (a rare, early-onset form of ALS), lead to toxic gain-of-function activity of FUS protein in a FUS-ALS mouse model. As a result, Dr. Shneider and his research team collaborated with Ionis Pharmaceuticals to develop jacifusen (a.k.a., ulefnersen, ION363), an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) drug aimed to lower the expression of FUS and thereby reduce FUS-mediated toxicity in patients. In 2019, Dr. Shneider launched the first expanded access program to treat patients suffering with FUS-ALS with jacifusen. Following the safety and possible efficacy observed in this initial program, jacifusen is currently being further evaluated in a global phase 1-3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Dr. Shneider’s research and clinical works have enabled additional ongoing clinical trials involving participants with various forms of ALS, including the recent Silence ALS initiative which aims to design individualized, gene-silencing therapies for patients with ALS. Please see our Clinical Trials page for more information.

Dr. Shneider graduated with an AB from Harvard College and an MD-PhD from Columbia University and was Chief Resident of the Harvard Longwood Neurology Training Program. He completed his graduate work in the laboratory of Dr. Richard Axel and his postdoctoral fellowship in the laboratory of Dr. Thomas Jessell. After several years in the Intramural Program of the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke, Dr. Shneider returned to Columbia University as Assistant Professor of Neurology in the Neuromuscular Division. He is a member of the Center for Motor Neuron Biology and Disease and of the H. Houston Merritt Clinical Research Center. Dr. Shneider has clinical expertise in the diagnosis and care of patients with ALS and related motor neuron diseases. Outside of the laboratory and clinic, Dr. Shneider enjoys modern art and is a frequent visitor of the MoMA.
