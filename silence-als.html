Silence ALS

Silence ALS is the lab's antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapeutics program, built around a simple idea: if a mutant gene product is toxic to motor neurons, lowering its expression before it accumulates can slow or halt disease.

Antisense oligonucleotide therapy

From target to treatment: ulefnersen for FUS-ALS

In FUS-ALS, a mutant FUS protein accumulates in motor neurons and becomes toxic. Ulefnersen (ION363, originally named jacifusen) is an antisense oligonucleotide that binds FUS pre-mRNA directly and directs its degradation — lowering production of the toxic protein before it can harm the cell, rather than treating downstream damage after it has occurred.

The approach grew out of the lab's basic work on how mutant FUS causes selective motor neuron toxicity, and reflects a broader strategy the lab takes with genetic forms of ALS: identify the toxic gene product, then silence it directly at the RNA level.

Antisense oligonucleotides Nature Medicine, 2022 The Lancet, 2025

Program milestones

Ulefnersen's development, from a validated target to an ongoing global clinical trial.

01

Target validation in a FUS knock-in mouse model

The lab established that mutant FUS causes selective motor neuron degeneration through a toxic gain of function, and showed in a mouse model that lowering mutant FUS expression could rescue the phenotype — the mechanistic basis for an ASO approach.

Mouse geneticsNature Communications, 2016
02

Compassionate-use treatment in a single patient

An early-generation ASO targeting FUS pre-mRNA was administered to a single patient with rapidly progressive FUS-ALS under compassionate use, providing the first clinical evidence that FUS silencing was achievable and tolerable in a person.

Compassionate useNature Medicine, 2022
03

Multicentre case series

An investigator-initiated, open-label case series extended treatment with ulefnersen (ION363, formerly jacifusen) to a broader group of FUS-ALS patients across multiple centers, reporting on safety and biomarker and clinical outcomes.

12-patient case seriesThe Lancet, 2025
04

Global clinical trial

Ulefnersen is now being evaluated in a global clinical trial for patients with FUS-ALS, building on the mouse, compassionate-use, and case-series data generated by the program.
