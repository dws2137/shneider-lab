Translating mechanism into medicine

Silencing toxic FUS with antisense oligonucleotides

In FUS-ALS, mutant FUS protein accumulates in motor neurons and becomes toxic. The lab helped develop ulefnersen (ION363, originally named jacifusen), an antisense oligonucleotide that binds FUS pre-mRNA and directs its degradation — lowering production of the toxic protein before it can harm the cell.

This approach was first validated in a FUS knock-in mouse model, then in a single patient treated under compassionate use, and most recently in a 12-patient case series. It is now being evaluated in a global clinical trial through the lab's Silence ALS program.
